# Nature backdrop videos: YouTube candidates and licensed loops

Research date: 2026-10-15. Purpose: a default "videos" catalog for projmap backdrops (projected behind products and
people, usually blurred), plus clips users can import as files.

## How this was checked

- **YouTube metadata** (title, channel, length, highest stream resolution, YouTube licence flag, description terms) was
  read from each watch page on 2026-10-15. "Verified" in the tables means those fields were read from the page.
- **Loop, sound and camera** were **not** checked by watching. They come from the title and description. Treat them as
  "probable" until someone watches the clip.
- **CC BY on YouTube** means the uploader chose "Creative Commons Attribution licence (reuse allowed)". YouTube uses
  CC BY 3.0. The uploader sets this flag themselves, so it proves nothing about who owns the footage. Several channels
  that set it are aggregators or contradict it in their own description. Those rows are flagged.
- **Possibly AI-generated** marks 2025-2026 uploads from "free loop" channels whose clips look generated (perfect
  "seamless loop 4K" scenes, weekly volume). One channel (Free Content Lab) labels its clips `[AI-Generated]`.
- **Stock-site clips** (Pexels, Pixabay, Mixkit, Wikimedia Commons) were found by search. Wikimedia rows were read from
  the Commons API (size, length, licence, author). Pexels and Pixabay block scripted fetches, so their creator names come
  from search snippets and resolution or length are not confirmed.

Credit line format for YouTube CC BY clips: `"<Title>" by <Channel> (youtube.com/watch?v=<id>), CC BY 3.0`.

## YouTube ToS: embedding vs downloading

- **Downloading is not allowed, even for CC BY videos.** The YouTube Terms of Service forbid accessing, reproducing or
  downloading content except "as expressly authorized by the Service" or "with prior written permission from YouTube
  and, if applicable, the respective rights holders". They also forbid circumventing measures that prevent copying.
  The CC BY flag is a copyright licence from the uploader. It does not lift the ToS. YouTube's own sanctioned route for
  CC reuse is its editor, not file download. A creator-hosted download (orangeHD, fireflyjack's Dropbox) is fine,
  because the file does not come from YouTube.
- **Embedding is allowed but does not fit projection mapping.** The YouTube API Developer Policies forbid apps that
  "modify, build upon, or block any portion or functionality of a YouTube player", "interfere with or obscure any
  attribution provided by YouTube", "separate, isolate, or modify the audio or video components", play from a
  background player that is not visible, or "download, import, backup, cache, or store copies of YouTube audiovisual
  content without YouTube's prior written approval". Blurring, warping onto surfaces, masking, or hiding the player
  chrome all count as modifying the player.
- **It also fails technically.** A cross-origin YouTube iframe cannot be drawn into a WebGL canvas or texture, so the
  renderer cannot blur or map it anyway.
- **What projmap can do with YouTube:** show a "Find more on YouTube" list of link-outs with credit, or play an
  unmodified embed in the editor preview only. Anything that goes through the renderer must be a file the user owns
  or one shipped under a licence like those above.
